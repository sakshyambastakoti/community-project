With an NPR 80,000–90,000 budget, I would redesign PRAYASH as a much stronger 9-day Rural Technology & Community Innovation Camp, rather than simply adding more components.

The extra budget should go toward better student kits, more hands-on projects, tools, community demonstrations, documentation, and take-home learning resources.

PROJECT PRAYASH 2026
9-Day Rural Technology & Community Innovation Camp

Dates: 28 October – 5 November 2026
Duration: 9 days
Target: Rural school students
Recommended Students: 40–50
Recommended Teams: 8–10 teams
Budget: NPR 85,000 approximately

The redesigned program keeps the original Project Prayash focus on technical education, community development, electrical safety and youth participation.

1. New Core Concept

Instead of:

Teach electronics → make a few projects

make the program:

Observe → Understand → Learn → Design → Build → Test → Deploy → Present

The students should work on real problems from their own school/community.

5. What Makes This Version Better?

The original concept includes technical training alongside structural development, awareness programs and community participation.

With the larger budget, I would make the technical component deeper and more sustainable.

Old Approach

Lecture → Small circuit → Certificate

Redesigned PRAYASH

Problem → Electronics → Programming → Prototype → Testing → Community Demonstration

6. Final Student Outcomes

After 9 days, a student should be able to:

Electronics
Identify common components.
Build basic circuits.
Use a breadboard.
Use a multimeter safely.
Understand basic sensors.
Programming
Understand basic Arduino programming.
Read sensor values.
Control LEDs, buzzers and servos.
Use simple conditions.
Engineering
Identify a problem.
Design a solution.
Select components.
Build a prototype.
Troubleshoot.
Test.
Social Impact

Most importantly, students should leave with the mindset:

"I can identify a problem around me and try to build a technological solution."

That is a much stronger outcome for a rural-development program than simply teaching students how to assemble Arduino projects.

7. Proposed PRAYASH Impact Target

For an NPR 80k–90k program, I would set measurable targets such as:

Target	Goal
Students trained	40–50
Student teams	8–10
Basic circuits built	40+
Mini projects	8–10
Final community projects	5–8
Technical mentors	4–6
Community exhibition	1
Students receiving certificates	40–50
Project documentation	8–10 project reports

The most important change is that the final projects should be selected from problems students actually observe locally, rather than bringing a fixed list of projects from Kathmandu. This makes the program much more defensible as a genuine rural community development initiative, consistent with PRAYASH's existing stated purpose.
