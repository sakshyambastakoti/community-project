SECTION 02

PROJECT OVERVIEW

RoboYuwa is a youth-led organization committed to empowering young people through technology, innovation, and community service. As part of our social responsibility initiative, Project PRAYASH is designed to improve educational facilities and promote basic electrical and electronics awareness in rural areas of Nepal.

Pillar 01

Technical Foundation

Students receive rigorous hands-on training in electricity, component electronics, sensor interfacing, microcontrollers (Arduino), basic programming, and strict electrical safety practices.

Pillar 02

Project Development

Moving beyond standard tutorials, students learn to identify real local problems, design tailored technological solutions, build physical prototypes, troubleshoot hardware/software, and document their work.

Pillar 03

Community Impact

Students apply their new skills to solve community issues relating to water management, energy conservation, sanitation, electrical safety, local agriculture, and school infrastructure.

SECTION 03

WHY TECHNICAL EDUCATION IN RURAL NEPAL?

Bridging the gap between theoretical schooling and practical technological empowerment.

The Challenge in Rural Schools

While science textbooks cover electricity and circuits theoretically, rural students rarely have access to breadboards, multimeters, sensors, or programmable microcontrollers. This creates a gap between textbook knowledge and practical problem-solving confidence.

The PRAYASH Solution

By bringing dedicated hardware kits, structured mentoring, and an engineering design mindset directly to rural government schools, PRAYASH demystifies technology. Students realize that technology is not an abstract luxury, but an accessible tool for improving their own community.

SECTION 04

PROGRAM OBJECTIVES

Structured dual-track learning goals spanning technical mastery and social impact.

Technical Objectives

  • Understand voltage, current, resistance, DC power, and electrical safety rules
  • Master breadboarding, component identification, and multimeter usage
  • Interface digital and analog sensors (LDR, IR, Ultrasonic, Temp, Rain, Water)
  • Program microcontrollers (Arduino) using variables, conditions, and loops
  • Build autonomous systems connecting Sensors → Controller → Outputs
  • Troubleshoot hardware wiring and software logic systematically

Social & Community Objectives

  • Conduct field problem discovery within local schools and village surroundings
  • Develop student team collaboration and project leadership skills
  • Promote local youth volunteerism and civic engagement
  • Demonstrate functional prototypes to parents, teachers, and municipality leaders
  • Foster long-term technological confidence among rural youth

SECTION 11

STUDENT TEAM STRUCTURE

Promoting collaborative learning through structured team roles.

Team Organization

4–5 Students Per Team (8–10 Total Teams)

To ensure every student gains genuine hands-on experience rather than remaining a passive spectator, students work in small teams with dedicated electronics kits. Roles rotate daily so all members experience every engineering phase.

Rotating Team Roles

  • Electronics Lead: Breadboarding and component connections
  • Programming Lead: Microcontroller code writing and tuning
  • Hardware Design Lead: Physical enclosure and sensor mounting
  • Documentation Lead: Circuit diagrams and testing logs
  • Presentation Lead: Team pitch spokesperson

SECTION 13

ELECTRICAL & WORKSHOP SAFETY

Dedicated safety protocols integrated into every practical workshop session.

Safety Standard

Low-Voltage Practical Work Only

Low Voltage Focus

All student experiments are strictly limited to safe low-voltage DC electricity (5V - 9V DC battery/USB power).

No Mains Experimentation

Students are explicitly prohibited from directly touching or experimenting with household/school AC mains electricity (230V AC).

Instructor Supervision

All circuit powering and multimeter testing is inspected by technical mentors before switches are turned ON.

Polarity Verification

Students learn polarity checking for capacitors, LEDs, and relays to prevent component damage or short circuits.

Tool Safety Handling

Safe handling rules for wire strippers, cutters, and component leads to avoid minor cuts or eye hazards.

Community Safety Awareness

Students learn basic household electrical safety rules to share with family members and rural neighbors.

OUTCOME 01

Understand basic electrical principles, component functions, sensor types, and electrical safety practices.

OUTCOME 02

BUILD

SECTION 18

COMMUNITY IMPACT

Three-tier realistic impact framework for rural community empowerment.

Tier 01

Student Level

Builds genuine technical self-confidence, unlocks practical STEM career exposure, and transforms students from passive technology consumers into active technological creators.

Tier 02

School Level

Elevates school science atmosphere, leaves reusable electronics equipment for ongoing student experimentation, and establishes a foundation for student robotics clubs.

Tier 03

Community Level

Encourages technology-based thinking toward local challenges in water, energy, and safety while fostering positive collaboration between youth, local schools, and ward leadership.

SECTION 19

FINAL TECH EXHIBITION (DAY 9)

PRAYASH TECH DAY: A public community event celebrating youth innovation.

Day 9 Event

BUILD → DEMONSTRATE → EXPLAIN → FEEDBACK

Students showcase their projects to teachers, parents, municipal representatives, local youth, and school peers. Each team gets 5 minutes (3 min explanation + 2 min live demo & Q&A).

Student Presentation Format

  • 3 Minutes: Explain problem statement, community impact, and working principle
  • 2 Minutes: Live physical project demonstration & audience Q&A

Exhibition Zones

ZONE 01
Basic Electronics

Demonstrating fundamental LED circuits, switches, buzzers, and multimeter measurement techniques.

ZONE 02
Smart Systems

Interactive sensor systems (LDR night lights, rain alarms, distance indicators, water level sensors).

ZONE 03
Community Solutions

Final team prototypes addressing local challenges in water, energy, sanitation, and school bell automation.

ZONE 04
Student Innovation

Creative student project ideas, technical posters, schematics, and future improvement proposals.
